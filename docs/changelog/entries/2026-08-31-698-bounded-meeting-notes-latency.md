### Make meeting-note generation bounded and predictable

- **Issue:** [#698](https://github.com/metagrover/pluto/issues/698)
- **PR:** [#702](https://github.com/metagrover/pluto/pull/702)
- **Changed:** Notes generation now records content-free queue, model, token, stage, repair, repartition, hierarchy, and stable failure-category metrics; sizes source leaves independently from concrete merges; retries and repartitions truncated work locally; retries one transiently failed hierarchy leaf without regenerating completed siblings; admits one primary meeting at a time with manual-first FIFO queueing; assigns explicit provider work classes; shows truthful queued versus generating status; and persists a two-attempt automatic retry cap for the same current source across app restarts. Manual retry and changed source inputs remain eligible.
- **Why:** Parallel regeneration previously hid queue behavior and could multiply model work. The bounded pipeline reduces avoidable hierarchy calls, keeps completed siblings during recovery, and makes wait time distinguishable from active model time without persisting meeting content. A production-history audit later found 24 expensive failures concentrated on one meeting, showing that restart-driven attempts also needed a durable cap.
- **Replaced:** Hypothetical merge reservations during leaf sizing, whole-run failure after a local truncation, implicit provider priorities, indistinguishable queued/generating status, restart-scoped retry memory, and reports that treated repeated attempts as independent corpus samples.
- **Notes:** Source grounding, writer/review contracts, publication revision checks, cancellation, node/depth ceilings, and prior-note preservation remain strict. Production audits and output budgets are unchanged. Organic reports now separate attempts from distinct meetings, count retry concentration and truncated stages, and compute latency only for published attempts without printing meeting identifiers. One legacy-source 30-minute candidate run published in 590,123 ms versus a failed 1,055,706 ms baseline, but three stricter 30-minute attempts all failed after 577,718–1,111,099 ms. A separate, benchmark-only final-audit experiment also failed during its third writer after 944,071 ms (three writer calls, zero audit/merge calls, 7 ms total queue time). This is not enough evidence for a representative average or the 50% median target, and performance promotion remains blocked.
